1A

The Vedas (वेदाः · Vedāḥ)

Origin · Structure · Four Vedas · Brāhmaṇas · Dialogue Hymns · Key Sūktas

Core Definition + Apaurūṣeyatva

Veda = root Vid + suffix Ghan = Knowledge (transcendental knowledge of Dharma). Vedas are Apauruṣeya — not authored by any human. Sages were Mantra-draṣṭāraḥ (seers, not creators). God revealed Vedas at the beginning of creation. Established by Mīmāṃsā philosophy.

विद् ज्ञाने धातोः घञ् — वेद = 'यो वेदयति धर्मादीन् स वेदः' Vid jñāne dhātoḥ ghaña — Veda = that which reveals Dharma Ṛkprātiśākhya · Sāyaṇācārya
मन्त्रब्राह्मणयोर्वेद नामधेयम् — (Apastamba) Veda = Mantras + Brāhmaṇas together
Overview — Four Vedas (चत्वारो वेदाः)

ऋग्वेद · Ṛgveda

  • Subject: Knowledge/Praise (Śāstram)
  • Priest: Hotā
  • 10 Maṇḍalas · 1028 Sūktas
  • 10,552 Mantras
  • Śākhās: Śākala, Bāṣkala
  • Brāhmaṇas: Aitareya, Kauṣītaki
  • Top deities: Indra (250), Agni (200), Soma (9th Maṇḍala)

यजुर्वेद · Yajurveda

  • Subject: Ritual Procedures (prose mantras)
  • Priest: Adhvaryu
  • Śukla (Āditya): Mādhyandina, Kāṇva
  • Kṛṣṇa (Brahma): Taittirīya, Maitrāyaṇī, Kaṭhaka
  • Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (largest)
  • Īśā Upaniṣad = 40th chapter
  • Vajasaneyī Saṃhitā = Śukla name

सामवेद · Sāmaveda

  • Subject: Music/Song (Sāma = Gīta)
  • Priest: Udgātā
  • Śākhās: Kauthuma, Rāṇāyanīya, Jaiminīya
  • Parts: Pūrvārcika + Uttarārcika
  • Most mantras from Ṛgveda
  • 7 musical notes origin (Sa Re Ga…)
  • Chāndogya Upaniṣad
  • Tāṇḍya Mahā Brāhmaṇa (Pañcaviṃśa)

अथर्ववेद · Atharvaveda

  • Subject: Medicine, Magic, Nation-building
  • Priest: Brahmā (Superintendent)
  • Also: Brahmaveda, Bhaijājyaveda, Kṣatraveda, Chandoveda, Aṅgiroveda
  • Śākhās: Śaunaka, Pippalāda
  • 20 Kāṇḍas
  • Gopatha Brāhmaṇa (only Brāhmaṇa)
  • Muṇḍaka, Praśna, Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣads

The Upavedas (उपवेदाः · Upavedāḥ)

Four practical sciences subordinate to the four Vedas

Definition

Upaveda = practical worldly knowledge associated with the Veda (Vedopakārakāḥ vidyāḥ). Bridges transcendental Vedic knowledge with applied sciences.

आयुर्वेदधनुर्वेदौ गान्धर्वश्चेति ते त्रयः। स्थापत्यवेदश्चतुर्थः उपवेदाः प्रकीर्तिताः॥ Āyurvedadhanurvēdau gāndharvāśceti te trayaḥ | Sthāpatyavedaścaturthaḥ upavedāḥ prakīrtitāḥ || Caraṇavyūha

Sthāpatyaveda — Architecture (Vāstuśāstra)
  • Three Temple Styles: Nāgara (North India — curvilinear Śikhara) · Drāviḍa (South — pyramid Vimāna + Gopuram) · Vesara (Central — hybrid of both)
  • Key Texts: Mānasāra · Samārāṅgaṇasūtradhāra · Māyamataṃ · Aparājitapṛcchā

The Vedāṅgas (वेदाङ्गाः · Vedāṅgāḥ)

Six auxiliary sciences — Limbs of the Veda · Without these, Vedic study is impossible

Why Needed?

Vedic mantras are highly ancient. Without Vedāṅgas, correct pronunciation (Śikṣā), grammatical form (Vyākaraṇa), meaning (Nirukta), meter (Chanda), ritual procedure (Kalpa), and cosmic timing (Jyotiṣa) are impossible.

षडङ्गाणि वेदस्य। ब्राह्मणो निष्कारणो धर्मः षडङ्गो वेदोऽध्येयो ज्ञेयश्च। Mahābhāṣya (Patañjali)

1. Śikṣā
Phonetics		 Correct pronunciation of letters, accents (Udātta, Anudātta, Svarita), duration Pāṇinīyaśikṣā · Yājñavalkyaśikṣā 8 places of articulation: chest, throat, head, tongue-root, teeth, nose, lips, palate. 6 merits of reciter: sweetness, clarity, word-separation, good pitch, steadiness, rhythm. 6 faults: singing, rushing, head-shaking, blind reading, ignorance, weak voice.

3. Vyākaraṇa
Grammar		 Word analysis, grammatical correctness, root+suffix derivation Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī (3,995 sūtras, 8 chapters) · Patañjali's Mahābhāṣya Munitraya: Pāṇini (core rules) → Kātyāyana (Vārtikas — amendments) → Patañjali (Mahābhāṣya — commentary). 5 Purposes: Rakṣā (protect Veda) · Ūha (ritual modification) · Āgama (scriptural duty) · Laghu (easy learning) · Asaṃdeha (remove doubt). 14 Śiva Sūtras → 42 Pratyāhāras.
4. Nirukta
Etymology		 Meanings of difficult Vedic words; etymology (Nirvacana) Nirukta by Sage Yāska; based on Nighaṇṭu dictionary 4 Parts of Speech: Nāma (noun/substance-primary) · Ākhyāta (verb/action-primary) · Upasarga (prefix — illuminates meaning) · Nipāta (particle — comparative, conjunctive, expletive). 3 Deity Realms: Terrestrial (Agni) · Atmospheric (Vāyu/Indra) · Celestial (Sūrya). Famous: "One who reads without understanding is a burden-carrier (Sthāṇurayaṃ bhārahāraḥ…)"
5. Chanda
Meter		 Counting syllables and beats (Mātrā) in Vedic mantras Chandaḥsūtram by Sage Piṅgala (Piṅgalanāga) 7 Vedic meters (each +4 syllables): Gāyatrī(24) · Uṣṇik(28) · Anuṣṭubh(32) · Bṛhatī(36) · Paṅkti(40) · Triṣṭubh(44) · Jagatī(48). Piṅgala's 8-group formula: Ya-ma-ta-rā-ja-bhā-na-sa-la-gā. Akṣara-vṛtta (syllable count) vs Mātrā-vṛtta (duration count).
6. Jyotiṣa
Astronomy		 Timing of Vedic rituals; astronomical calculations for rituals Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa by Sage Lagadha (oldest text) Calculates: Solstices, seasons, months, Tithi (lunar days), Nakṣatras (constellations). Ahargaṇa = total elapsed days from cosmic epoch (Kalpa). Foundation of Indian astronomy. "Jyotiṣa is the eyes of the Veda."
Śulba Sūtra — Geometry & Mathematics

'Śulba' = measuring cord/rope. Used to build geometrically precise fire-altars (Vedis) in shapes like falcon, circle, square. Contains Baudhayana's Theorem — antecedent of the Pythagorean theorem:

दीर्घचतुरस्रस्याक्ष्णया रज्जुः पार्श्वमानी तिर्यङ्मानी च यत् पृथग्भूते कुरुतस्तदुभयं करोति। Dīrghacaturasrasyākṣṇayā rajjuḥ pārśvamānī tiryaṅmānī ca yat pṛthagbhūte kurutastadubhayaṃ karoti | Baudhāyana Śulbasūtra 1.48

Modern equivalent: "The diagonal of a rectangle produces both areas that its length and breadth produce separately." = a² + b² = c²

Yāska's Famous Dictum (Nirukta)
स्थाणुरयं भारहारः किलाभूदधीत्य वेदं न विजानाति योऽर्थम्। Sthāṇurayaṃ bhārahāraḥ kilābhūd adhītya vedaṃ na vijānāti yo'rtham | Nirukta 1.18

"He who has studied the Veda but does not know its meaning is like a post carrying a heavy burden." — Underscores the absolute necessity of Nirukta.

Daśopaniṣads (दशोपनिषद् · Daśopaniṣad)

Principal Ten Upaniṣads — Vedānta philosophy — Brahmavidyā

Definition + Subject

Upaniṣad = Upa (near) + ni (down) + ṣad (sit) = Knowledge received sitting near the Guru that destroys ignorance (Avidyānāśakam) and leads to Brahman (Brahmagamakam).

Subject: Unity of Jīva (individual soul) + Brahman (Universal Soul) + Jagat (Universe). Attainment of Mokṣa. This is the philosophy of Vedānta — the culmination of the Vedas.

108 total Upaniṣads. 10 principal = Daśopaniṣads (commented by Ādi Śaṅkarācārya). Vedas: Mantra + Brāhmaṇa + Āraṇyaka + Upaniṣad.

उप + नि + सद् — अविद्यानाशकम् ब्रह्मगमकं ज्ञानम्। वेदान्तो नाम उपनिषत्प्रमाणम्।Vedāntasāra

3. Kaṭha Upaniṣad (कठोपनिषद्)
Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · Kaṭha Śākhā
FatherVajāśravas (Naciketa's father)
3 Boonsfrom Yama
Key themeEternal Ātman · Chariot metaphor · Śreya vs Preya
  • 3rd Boon: "Tell me the secret of death." Yama offers wealth, kingdoms, women — Naciketa refuses all. Truly dispassionate (Vairāgya).
  • Śreya vs Preya: Śreya = path of ultimate welfare/liberation. Preya = path of worldly pleasure. "A wise person chooses Śreya though it is difficult."
  • Nature of Ātman: "Aṇor aṇīyān mahato mahīyān" — subtler than the subtlest, greater than the greatest. Eternal — never born, never dies.
  • Chariot Metaphor (Ratha-rūpaka): Body = chariot. Soul = Rathī (owner). Intellect (Buddhi) = charioteer. Mind = reins. Senses = horses. Sense-objects = roads. Control senses → liberation.

4. Praśna Upaniṣad (प्रश्नोपनिषद्)
Atharvaveda · Pippalāda Śākhā
6 Sagesask 6 questions to Pippalāda
Key themesPrāṇa · Rāyi · Ṣoḍaśakāla Puruṣa
  • Q1 — Creation: Prajāpati created Rāyi (matter/moon/food) and Prāṇa (energy/sun/consciousness). All life born from their union.
  • Q2 — Supremacy of Prāṇa: Prāṇa is greatest of all — sustains life, gods, senses. "Like bees leaving when the queen leaves, all senses depart when Prāṇa goes."
  • Q3 — Five Prāṇas: Apāna (lower organs), Prāṇa (eyes/nose/mouth), Samāna (navel), Vyāna (all 72,000 nāḍīs), Udāna (Suṣumnā — upward at death).
  • Q4 — States of consciousness: In waking and dreaming, Prāṇa is active. In deep sleep, everything merges into Prāṇa.
  • Q5 — OM meditation: A = human world · U = lunar world · A+U+M = Solar world (Brahma Loka).
  • Q6 — 16-part Puruṣa (Ṣoḍaśakāla): Resides within this very body.

5. Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad (मुण्डकोपनिषद्)
Atharvaveda · Śaunaka Śākhā
Source ofIndia's national motto
Key themesParā/Aparā Vidyā · Two Birds · Fragile boats
  • Two Vidyās: Aparā Vidyā = Vedas + Vedāṅgas (worldly, lower knowledge). Parā Vidyā = knowledge of imperishable Supreme Brahman (higher, liberating knowledge).
  • Fragile boats: Desire-driven rituals = "Adriḍhāḥ Plavāḥ" — fragile, leaky boats that cannot carry one across the ocean of saṃsāra.
  • Spider metaphor: As a spider spins and withdraws its web; as herbs grow on earth — so the universe originates from Brahman.
  • Two Birds metaphor: Jīvātman (individual soul) eats fruits of karma and grieves. Paramātman simply watches as a witness. Realizing Paramātman → liberation.
  • Jīvanmukti: "Bhidyate hṛdayagranthiḥ…" — Knot of heart-ignorance breaks, all doubts severed, past karmas exhausted → liberation like rivers merging into ocean.

Key theme4 states of Ātman · OM = A+U+M

7. Taittirīya Upaniṣad (तैत्तिरीयोपनिषद्)
Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda · Taittirīya Āraṇyaka
3 VallisŚikṣā · Brahmānanda · Bhṛgu
Key themePañcakoṣa · Ānaṃda-mīmāṃsā · Bhṛgu-Varuṇa
  • Convocation address (Śikṣā Vallī): "Satyaṃ vada. Dharmaṃ cara. Svādhyāyān mā pramadaḥ." Revere mother, father, teacher, guest as God.
  • Pañcakoṣas (5 Sheaths): Annamaya → Prāṇamaya → Manomaya → Vijñānamaya → Ānandamaya (outermost to innermost). True Ātman lies beyond all five.
  • Definition of Brahman (Bhṛgu Vallī): "That from which all beings are born, by which they are sustained, into which they merge = Brahman."
  • Ānanda-mīmāṃsā: Scale of bliss from human joy to Prajāpati's joy — but ultimate bliss = Brahmānanda (infinite bliss), naturally experienced by the desireless knower of Brahman.

9. Chāndogya Upaniṣad (छान्दोग्योपनिषद्)
Sāmaveda · Chāndogya Brāhmaṇa
MahāvākyaTat Tvam Asi
Key dialoguesUddalaka–Śvetaketu · Satyakāma Jābāla · Sanatkumāra–Nārada
  • Tat Tvam Asi (6th Prapāṭhaka): Uddalaka teaches Śvetaketu. Trivṛtkaraṇa: From one Sat → Fire, Water, Earth (each contains all three). "Salt in water" metaphor — Brahman pervades all but unseen. Tat = Brahman · Tvam = Jīva · Asi = "You are" → non-duality.
  • Satyakāma Jābāla: Honestly revealed unknown lineage. Sage Gautama: "Only a true Brahmin would speak such truth." → Truth is highest virtue.
  • Sarvam khalvidam Brahma (Śāṇḍilya Vidyā): "This entire universe is Brahman — Tajjalān (born from, merges into, breathes in Brahman)."
  • Bhūma Vidyā: "Where one sees nothing else, hears nothing else = Bhūma (the Infinite)." True happiness only in the Infinite, not in the finite.
  • Dahara Vidyā: Brahman resides in the subtle space (Dahara Ākāśa) within the lotus of the heart in the "city of Brahman" (the body).
  • Pañcāgni Vidyā: 5 cosmic fires — Heaven, Rain, Earth, Man, Woman. Wise (Devayana path → Brahma Loka). Ritualists only (Pitṛyāna path → rebirth).
  • Indra-Virochana story: Materialism (Asura-buddhi) = identifying the physical body as the self. Indra goes back to Prajāpati 4 times over 101 years to learn the true spiritual Self.

10. Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad (बृहदारण्यकोपनिषद्)
Śukla Yajurveda · Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa
Largest Upaniṣad
MahāvākyaAhaṃ Brahmāsmi
Key themesNeti Neti · Yājñavalkya · Gārgī · Maitreī · Da-Da-Da
  • Ahaṃ Brahmāsmi (1.4.10): "In the beginning this was Brahman alone. It knew itself — 'I am Brahman.' Therefore it became all." The foundational experience of non-duality.
  • Neti Neti (2.3.6): Brahman is infinite and attribute-less → cannot be described positively. Only approach: "Not this, not this" — negate all limiting adjuncts. Yājñavalkya's method.
  • Yājñavalkya–Maitreyī dialogue (2.4): "Everything is dear not for its own sake but for the sake of the Self (Ātman)." The Ātman alone must be seen, heard, reflected upon, meditated upon — Śravaṇa, Manana, Nididhyāsana.
  • Gārgī–Yājñavalkya (3.8): Female scholar Gārgī asks about ultimate foundation of universe. Yājñavalkya reveals the Akṣara Brahman (the Imperishable) — by whose command sun, moon, seasons are regulated.
  • Da-Da-Da (5.2): Prajāpati's single instruction "Da" to three types: Gods → Damyata (self-control). Humans → Datta (give in charity). Demons → Dayadhvam (show compassion).
  • Jyotir-mīmāṃsā (4.3): King Janaka asks what is one's light when sun, moon, fire, speech have all quieted. Yājñavalkya: "The Self alone is one's light."
  • Madhu Vidyā (2.5): Elements of nature are mutually beneficial like honey (Madhu). Behind all diversity = one immortal Self (Brahman).

Mahāpurāṇas (महापुराणाः)

18 Great Purāṇas — Mythology · Cosmology · Dynastic History · Bhakti · Philosophy

Key Facts

Author: Mahāṛṣi Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana Vedavyāsa. Total Ślokas: 4,00,000. Largest: Skanda Purāṇa (81,000). First: Brahma Purāṇa (Ādi Purāṇa). Made Vedic knowledge accessible to women and all social classes.

Every true Mahāpurāṇa must contain all five
1. SargaPrimary creation — Brahma creates Universe from Brahman
2. PratisargaSecondary recreation after cosmic dissolution (Pralaya/Mahāpralaya)
3. VaṃśaGenealogy of gods and sages from creation onward
4. Manvantara14 Manu periods (~4.32 billion years each) — cosmic time cycles
5. VaṃśānucāritaDynastic history of Solar (Sūrya) and Lunar (Chandra) royal lineages

Sāttvika
6 Aṃśas. Parāśara-Maitreya dialogue. Creation sequence: Brahman+Prakṛti+Kāla → Mahat → Ahaṃkāra. Best for dynastic history.

Sāttvika
12 Skandhas. 10th = Kṛṣṇa's full life. Navadha Bhakti (Śravaṇa, Kīrtana, etc.). Foundation of Vaiṣṇavism.

Sāttvika
Vaiṣṇava dharma. Detailed description of 6 Vedāṅgas. Various Vratas.
4. Garuḍa Purāṇa
Sāttvika
Preta Khaṇḍa (afterlife/funeral rites). Karma-Vipāka (fruition of deeds). Āyurveda. Veterinary science for horses.
5. Padma Purāṇa
Sāttvika
5–7 Khaṇḍas. Pilgrimage glory. Rāma's life in Pātāla Khaṇḍa (post-Rāvaṇa events). Śāligrāma-Tulasī glory.
6. Varāha Purāṇa
Sāttvika
Varāha avatar. Mathurā Māhātmya — all sins destroyed by bathing there. Viśrāma Ghaṭ, Govardhan.
7. Brahma Purāṇa
Rājasika
ĀDI PURĀṆA (oldest). Jagannāth Purī (Puruṣottama Kṣetra, Odisha). Konāditya region (Konark). 12 names of Sun.

Rājasika
Lalitā Sahasranāma (Lalitā Tripurasundarī). Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa in Uttara Khaṇḍa. Bhaṇḍāsura story.

Rājasika
Rādhā = Kṛṣṇa's Hlādinī Śakti (not just a Gopī). Goloka Rāsa Līlā. "Kṛṣṇastu Bhagavān svayam."

Rājasika
Durgā Saptaśatī (Devī Māhātmyam, 13 chapters). Mahiṣāsura, Śumbha-Niśumbha slaying. Jaimini-Dharmabird dialogue.

Rājasika

Rājasika
Vāmana (dwarf) avatar. King Bali story — 3 paces cover the cosmos, Bali sent to Pātāla.

Tāmasika
Śaivism. Śiva's various forms. Rudra worship.
14. Liṅga Purāṇa
Tāmasika
12 Jyotirliṅgas. Liṅgodbhava story — Śiva as endless pillar of fire; Brahmā and Viṣṇu could not find ends. Śiva = Supreme Brahman.

Tāmasika

Tāmasika

  • Dynastic history: Matsya + Vāyu + Viṣṇu Purāṇas (most reliable)

  • Pāśupata Yoga: Vāyu Purāṇa (Śaiva tradition)
  • Kali Yuga signs: Viṣṇu Purāṇa 6th Aṃśa — wealth = sole criterion of greatness, rulers become looters, Dharma declines
  • Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa: Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa (Uttara Khaṇḍa)
  • Jagannāth Puri (Puruṣottama Kṣetra): Brahma + Skanda Purāṇas
4B

Upapurāṇas (उपपुराणाः)

18 Secondary Purāṇas — regional, sectarian, supplementary

Difference from Mahāpurāṇas

Mahāpurāṇas: Pan-Indian, composed by Vedavyāsa, fully contain Pañcalakṣaṇa. Upapurāṇas: Secondary, composed by various sages (not exclusively Vyāsa), often regional, preserve local pilgrimage traditions and sectarian Śaiva/Vaiṣṇava/Śākta practices.

Most Famous Upapurāṇa: Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa — glorifies the Divine Mother (Śakti tradition). Some scholars consider it a Mahāpurāṇa.

  • 18 Upapurāṇas include: Sanatkumāra, Narasiṃha, Bṛhan-Nāradīya, Śiva-Dharma, Durvāsa, Kapila, Vāmana, Bhārgava, Varuṇa, Kālikā, Sāmba, Nandi, Sūrya, Parāśara, Vasiṣṭha, Devī-Bhāgavata, Gaṇeśa, Haṃsa
  • Devī-Bhāgavata: 18,000 verses. 12 Skandhas. Full glorification of Śakti (Devī). Foundation of Śāktism.
  • Significance: Preserve sectarian traditions, regional Tīrtha-māhātmyas, and local mythology not covered by Mahāpurāṇas.

Smṛtis (स्मृतयः · Smṛtayaḥ)

18 Law Books — codifying Dharma for society across the Yugas

Śruti vs Smṛti + 4 Sources of Dharma

Śruti = "heard" directly from God (Vedas) — highest authority. Smṛti = "remembered/recollected" tradition codified by sages — based on Śruti. In any conflict: Śrutireva garīyasī (Śruti always wins).

All 18 Smṛtis — Key Details
1. Manusmṛti
Sage Manu · 12 chapters (Adhyāyas)
Oldest & most authoritative. 16 Saṃskāras (Garbhādhāna → Antyeṣṭi). 8 types of marriage. Rājadharma (Ch.7): king = 8 divine elements. 18 Law Titles. Foundation of Hindu law.
2. Yājñavalkya Smṛti
Yājñavalkya · 3 Kāṇḍas
Ācāra + Vyavahāra (civil/criminal law) + Prāyaścitta. More systematic than Manu. Basis of modern Hindu Law. Mitākṣarā commentary by Vijñāneśvara.
3. Parāśara Smṛti
Sage Parāśara
For Kali Yuga — most lenient/flexible. Allows agriculture for Brāhmaṇas in crisis (Āpad-dharma). Simplifies atonements.
4. Viṣṇu Smṛti
Prose/Gadya-Sūtra style (unique)
Framed as Viṣṇu-Earth (Pṛthvī) dialogue. 14 types of charity. Pilgrimage glory. Śrāddha rituals.
5. Hārita Smṛti
Sage Hārita
Women's Vedic education supported! Two categories: Brahmavādinīs (lifelong Vedic scholars) & Sadyovadhūs (who choose marriage). Historical proof of women's right to education.
6. Bṛhaspati Smṛti
Bṛhaspati
Best for Vyavahāra (jurisprudence). 4 types of courts. Witness rules. Documentary evidence. Divine ordeals. Sophisticated legal framework.
7. Kātyāyana Smṛti
Kātyāyana
Best for Strīdhana (women's property rights) — wealth received at marriage/gifts = wife's exclusive property, husband cannot claim it.
8. Nārada Smṛti
Nārada
Exclusively a legal procedural code. Judge qualifications. Witness rules. Laws on slave emancipation. No conduct/atonement mix.
9. Aṅgiras Smṛti
Aṅgiras
Primarily Prāyaścitta (atonement) — for major sins like cow slaughter, forbidden relationships. Purification methods.
10. Atri Smṛti
Sage Atri
Dāna (charity), Japa (chanting), and Prāyaścitta. "Charity, charity, charity — results of charity."
11. Yama Smṛti
Yama
Śrāddha rituals, Naraka (hells), punishments of afterlife. Detailed Pitṛ Yajña rules.
12. Dakṣa Smṛti
Dakṣa
Householder stage (Gṛhastha Āśrama) superiority. Yoga and daily routine. "All other Āśramas depend on householder for survival."
13. Saṃvarta Smṛti
Saṃvarta
Rules of Brahmacarya Āśrama (student life) in detail.
14. Vyāsa Smṛti
Vyāsa
Varṇāśrama duties, Dāna, Vedānta knowledge. "Dharma, Artha, Kāma, Mokṣa."
15. Uśanas Smṛti
Śukrācārya (son of Bhṛgu)
Origin of mixed castes (Saṃkarajāti) via Anuloma-Pratiloma marriages. Chandāla from Brāhmaṇī+Śūdra union.
16. Śaṅkha-Likhita
Two brothers
Rule of Law above even the King. Famous story: Śaṅkha as judge ordered Likhita's (own brother's) hand cut off for minor theft → law is fully impartial.
17. Āpastamba Smṛti
Āpastamba · Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda
Oldest surviving Dharmasūtra for Taittirīya tradition. Foundation of Dharmaśāstra.
18. Gautama Smṛti
Gautama · Sāmaveda
Oldest surviving Dharmasūtra. 40 Saṃskāras. Property + inheritance + civil law basics.

Nītiśāstra & Nyāyaśāstra (नीतिशास्त्रम्)

Statecraft · Political Science · Ethics · Logic — Arthaśāstra · Pañcatantra · Nyāya

Nītiśāstra — Definition

The science of polity, statecraft, ethics, and practical wisdom for governance and daily life. Two main pillars: Kauṭilya's Arthaśāstra (complete statecraft) and Viṣṇu Śarma's Pañcatantra (practical wisdom through stories). Compared to Machiavelli's The Prince — but more comprehensive and ethical.

Kauṭilya's Arthaśāstra
Overview

Author: Kauṭilya = Cāṇakya = Viṣṇugupta. Minister to Candragupta Maurya. Structure: 15 Adhikaraṇas (books), 150 chapters, 180 sections. Rediscovered by R. Śāmāśāstrī in 1909 (a manuscript in Mysore).

Pañcatantra — Viṣṇu Śarma's 5 Books
Purpose & Global Impact

Composed by Viṣṇu Śarma to teach Nītiśāstra to the dull princes of King Amaraśakti through engaging animal stories. Most widely translated Indian text after the Vedas — 50+ languages. Arabic = Kalīla wa-Dimna (8th century). Reached Europe as Fables of Bidpai.

Book 1
Mitrabhedaḥ
How friends are separated — beware of those who sow division. Story of Lion, Bull, and two jackals (Damanaka, Karaṭaka).
Book 2
Mitrasampraptiḥ
Gaining unlikely friends — cooperation overcomes obstacles. Story of Crow, Mouse, Deer, and Tortoise (Laghupatanikā, Hiraṇyaka, Citrāṅga, Mantharaka).
Book 3
Kākol-ūkīyam
War between crows and owls — strategy, intelligence, deception in conflict. Enemy within is most dangerous.
Book 4
Labdhapranāśaḥ
Loss of what is gained — do not lose through greed what you have already secured.
Book 5
Aparīkṣitakārakaḥ
Rash action without thinking — think before acting; the Brāhmin who killed the mongoose story.
Other Nītiśāstra Texts
  • Śukranīti (Nīti Prakāśikā): By Śukrācārya — kingship, economy, army, punishment. Includes 4-fold classification of weapons (Mukta, Amukta, Muktāmukta, Yantramukta).
  • Kāmandakīya Nītisāra: By Kāmandaka — based on Arthaśāstra. 20 cantos. Poetic exposition of statecraft.
  • Hitopadeśa: By Nārāyaṇa Paṇḍita — similar to Pañcatantra. 4 books. Very popular in Bengal. More focused on practical friendship.
  • Cāṇakya Nīti: Aphoristic sayings of Kauṭilya on practical wisdom for daily life, kingship, human nature.
  • Nītiśataka: By Bhartṛhari — 100 verses on practical ethics for all humans (not just kings).
Nyāyaśāstra (Unit 5C) — Logic & Epistemology
Nyāya System — Founded by Sage Gautama

Nyāya Sūtras by Gautama = foundational text. Subject = Tarka (logic), Pramāṇa (valid knowledge), and Vāda (debate). Goal = attain liberation through correct knowledge.

  • Navya-Nyāya: Founded by Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya (12–13th century) — more rigorous, technical logical analysis; influenced Indian and Western logic.
Jurisprudence in Smṛtis (Nīti Grantha aspect)
  • Manusmṛti Ch.8: 18 Titles of Law (Vivāda Padāni): recovery of debts, deposits, sales without ownership, assault, verbal abuse, theft, robbery, boundary disputes, etc.
  • Yājñavalkya Smṛti — Vyavahāra Kāṇḍa: Most systematic civil/criminal law. Loans, inheritance, border disputes, women's property. Foundation of modern Hindu Law.
  • Nārada Smṛti: Exclusively legal code. Judge qualifications: Śrotriya (Vedic scholar), noble lineage, impartial, knowledgeable in all Śāstras.
  • Bṛhaspati Smṛti: 4 types of courts — Kulaṃ (family council), Śreṇī (guild), Gaṇa (assembly), Adhikṛta (king's court). Evidence = Likhita (written documents) + Bhukti (possession) + Sākṣiṇaḥ (witnesses).
  • Kātyāyana Smṛti — Strīdhana definition: "Wealth received at marriage from parents, in-laws, husband, or brothers is Strīdhana — her exclusive property." (Husband cannot claim it.)
